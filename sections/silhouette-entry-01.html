Beef Season 2 Episode 1

April 2026

In case your Netflix masthead did not tell you, the second season of the no-longer-a-hidden-gem show is out. Lee Sung Jin is back with another edition of my top TV show of 2023.

Oscar Isaac and Carey Mulligan reunite. It wasn't that long ago that I watched 'Inside Llewyn Davis' and they carry the same dynamics - her being holier-than-thou but not as accomplished (+ a British accent in Beef), him talented but insufferable. As much as I am delighted that season 2 is a completely different storyline and not some painfully obvious cash-grab extension of season 1, it is hard not to compare it with season 1. The smarts and imagery of season 1 have been retained (the almost road crash was a nice little callback without being on the nose)

But - Beef season 1 had a lightness about it, the levity that comes from Steven Yeun feeling alive when Ali Wong chases him. The scene with Young Mazino and Ali Wong jumping on the bed after weeks of catfishing was poetic respite in the tense revenge. It might be harsh to judge one episode against an entire season but so far, the lack of whimsy is a downgrade (The only exception is the scene where Oscar Isaac has his golf club raised and the naive-in-love couple stare for a second before running away straight - the Edgar Wright / Buster Keaton effect) Anxiety only works when there are moments to show what peace looks like. Otherwise, it is just tedious.

One of the surprises was calling Mulligan / Crane's aesthetic sense as colonial. It does a lot - South Korea's own history with colonialism from Japan, and a commentary on how "traditional" aesthetics are viewed in a modern, multi-cultural world. It could sprawl into its own theme in the season. But there's too much already at play: commentaries and consequences of late-stage capitalism, complexities of old relationship, fragility of new ones and 4 main characters. While the jury is still out on the number of protagonists, they are all surprisingly fleshed out well in a 40-minute episode.

With 8 episodes this season, can they explore all of them well or are they going to hint and leave?

PS: Oscar Isaac is delightful as always, even when he is an a-hole.
