Longing

July 2026

I have an uneasy relationship with longing - the yearning, the wanting to just wait. Uneasy is just me trying not to wound the romantics - I hate it. It feels queasy in my stomach. You would think that a notion that has inspired so many poems and songs and stories would be held better in the mind of a writer but no, not in mine.

I think if you take away all the facades of love and sacrifice from longing, ultimately, it is a passive act. A state of biding your time - avoiding any responsibility for what happens, what you choose to let happen.

As with most things, it probably started with my mother. The woman who has been mythologized for her sheer will to live with my father all her married life - in spite of the abuse and the violence and the depletion of wealth before they hit early-30s, before the age that I am when I write this. Hers was, is, a story of nobility, of unending loyalty that somehow also invited pity when needed. What sustained the moral veneer was an endless tirade about compromises and endurance, day-in day-out. Why would an educated woman from a family of financial and social means decide to spend her entire life keeping this tale up while those who lived with her had to accompany her on this supposed hapless journey? I have asked this question for 32 years and I do not have an answer yet. That is to say, she is still with him, caring for him through Parkinson's and quibbles about why he put a clean fork in the sink as dirty. The helpless wife.

I have an aversion to helplessness more than other things I hate in this world (total of which I can count on one hand). I am suspicious of it. I reject it faster than any other uncomfortable feelings I am capable of experiencing. I was watching the anime Baccano! and the show has every shade of morally grey characters. Still I hate the whiny Jacuzzi Splot, having to constantly be emotionally managed, more than Ladd Russo, a literal psychopath. At least the psychopath owns who he is.

This is not me being heartless. When Steven McKeller sings "One of these days letters are gonna fall from the sky, telling us all to go free. But until that day, I'll find a way to let everybody know that you're coming back - you're coming back for me", it moves me in a way that defies explanation. I would stop everything else to listen to it when I was 22, I still do at 32.

When Joan Didion stands in front of her dead husband's closet, prompted to clear the clothes and shoes out of it and she says, "But what if he comes back?" tears roll down my cheek even knowing that their marriage had a steady undercurrent of ego and jealousy that she absorbed throughout. What a frail sentence from a fierce woman.

I am not conceited enough to say that things don't happen to me. This is not the withdrawal of a control freak. But the act of giving up agency is not one that sits well with me, not after everything I (and women everywhere) fought to get that agency in the first place. And to waste it on an act of the unrequited, however precious it might be.

I hate longing. But not without hating myself.
