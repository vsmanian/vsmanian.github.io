Wind in Your Hair

December 2025

Leaving the gray skies of England behind, we arrived at the land of mint tea, zellige, and congregation of cats. Morocco is now officially our annual destination for some December sun. We clear immigration and customs in record 15-minutes and deal with logistics while my head is buzzing with snippets from Butter — the only book that managed to get my full attention this year. All the monologues about women glorifying sacrifice and diminishing themselves to the point of non-existence, juxtaposed with my observation of social hierarchies established by the women in my family flicked through my head (What is vacation if not for confronting your existential crisis?) I get into the taxi, put my passport away, and let my mind wander while my eyes soak in the pink walls of Marrakech.

Have you ever had a Ratatouille moment? Like the one from the movie where one image, one smell, one bite is all it takes to flood your head with memories that you didn't realize you'd buried? Memories that feel like both from yesterday and from an era past? I had one of mine at 8:32 pm on December 4, 2025. Mindlessly staring outside the window of my taxi, I see other cars and vans, all probably with tourists like us.

And then there were two women, clearly not tourists. They were on a scooter, one riding pillion, feeding the other woman something from a box. There was something ridiculously joyous about them — you could see their laugh, their carefree faces, and the wind in their hair. Not that long ago, that right there was my definition of freedom, the ultimate cool factor — being able to go where you wanted, especially when the sun was down, in a scooter that symbolised style and confidence. You still had constraints and worries and people wanting you to "behave like a girl" but not when you were on your scooter. Riding your scooter was a high you didn't think existed in the world or one you had access to. When you rode, you defined your rules.

Living for the last 6 years in a country that preferred public transport and cars, it was so easy to gloss over the fact that I now had the freedom I once craved for. Career and life transitions meant it was easy to move the goal post. The two women now rode past us — and all the debate about pecking order, promotions, and my place in the world quietened, for I could feel the wind in my hair.
