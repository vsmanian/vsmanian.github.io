Diary Saha

April 2026

'Diary' Saha passed away today. I am one of the handful of people who always had mixed feelings about him but his influence on how I viewed radio, music, and Tamil lyrics is undeniable. When Hello FM started in Trichy, it was a new wave in the air that only held the government channel till then. Between Hello FM and Suriyan FM, both launched at the same time, Hello FM had a soul, it had a unique voice, due to people like Saha. His baritone voice, clear yet mellow enunciation, and the way he applied an almost analytical lens to Tamil song lyrics — dissecting them, expanding them, making time stand still so that the words could be savoured instead of just passing through a song — without losing the poetry and life, meant that he became a fan favourite in a small city like Trichy almost instantly. There were award shows, posters singing his praise — for the longest time, his face was a myth behind the voice that was everywhere. The one that explained the difference in pronunciation between ல, ள, and ழ in a way that rivalled Tamil scholars. But a talent like that could only be held in a small town for so long.

The time I no longer had the radio playing in the kitchen, I lost track of his professional journey but I still knew of him the way one knows of anyone on the internet. I know he rose up to head the Hello FM Trichy office, maybe more. I know he still stayed in Trichy predominantly, even though he got listenership from much farther.

Saha embodied the same flaw that many otherwise sharp thinkers from Tamil Nadu have, which is their difficulty separating the art from the artist. This primarily reflected in their stance on Vairamuthu, an acclaimed poet and a verified sex offender. The handful of times I met Saha in person, I could see the ruthlessness in the day that co-existed with the calming voice at night. I could acknowledge his talent, and still condemn his stance.

Today I got to know that he had a big celebration for his life's work just this January, probably knowing the limited time he had left. Goodbye Saha, thank you for turning the pages of your Diary with me.
