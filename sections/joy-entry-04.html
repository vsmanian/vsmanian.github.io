The Adult Thing To Do

January 2026

I walk around the temple, praying to every deity, asking them to help me deny this dread. If ever I needed proof that miracles could happen, it would be now. I was ready to cash the cosmic cheques my aunt had collected every year she fasted. "Anytime I really want something, I would pray to your ancestral god and it always comes true," I heard my mother's voice in my head. My body knew what my mind was struggling to accept.

*

Hour -15: I know this feeling, this fear. Something terrible has happened while I had the audacity to close my eyes, and now I have to wake up to its consequences. I see a missed call from my mother and call her back. At 8:11 am, I heard the news. At 10 am, I was on my way to the airport with a copy of my aunt's favourite book. I travel alone to her funeral, with my husband waiting for me to return with my soul intact.

*

Since moving to the UK, I have tried to stay in touch with people I cared about, people who cared about me, and people I found interesting. People don't always stay in the category they fall into. People had seasons. My aunt and I had the summer of our relationship for a year or two. We discussed books, movies, being transported to different worlds - physically or in our minds. I would speak to my aunt more in a day than I did with my mother over a week. Then the leaves fell and winter came. Somehow our world changed, priorities shifted, words hardened. While we still spoke, the time of the niece had passed.

*

Hour 0: There was something about the way my mother conveyed my aunt's passing that gave me the sense that I was not welcome. But I wanted to see her one last time, as myself. It was my duty, the adult thing to do. I also wanted to not perform, but grieve.

I landed at 1 pm the next day and realized that I had no cash or internet. I had at least an hour's car ride ahead of me. I stand in queues, ask around, overpay where I have to - as long as I get to the funeral at 4 pm. Bouts of crying and the South Indian sun made me dizzy.

*

"Amma, I'm hungry", my 5-year-old self asked my mother, the woman sitting on the floor. She was the dutiful wife, who watched as her husband spent his inheritance and her gold till there was none. "We have no rice in the house", my mother says with tears in her eyes. "Okay," I say and fall asleep on her lap.

*

Hour 3: I walk through the driveway that I had walked through about once a year for a decade now. People collect my backpack and I step foot in the house. I see someone I don't recognise in the open casket, while my cousin shouts at me, "This is why we didn't come to your wedding!" I apologise. My aunt was dead. It was the adult thing to do.

*

"Three, two, one, go!" I ran as fast as I could. My little white dress fluttering in the wind, I had the end line in sight. I was going to win this running race. I am now out of breath and see my brother and my cousin at the other end. They did not look like people who lost but like people having fun without me. "It was a slow race and our cousin won," I was told, my feet sore from the asphalt.

*

Hour 3, still: "Look at your aunt!", my mother demands at the open casket swarmed by flies. I hold my hands in respect. I had no flowers to offer – only myself, for the moment. I see my father and a few other familiar faces. "Where is AJ?" I ask. My mother marches me down to her. I was a prisoner being escorted to trial, when I had already agreed to the execution. AJ looks at me and turns away. I return to my aunt.

*

Hour 4: I sit next to my equally-socially-outcast dad, taking comfort in our misery. I hold his hands, partly to comfort, partly to mitigate his Parkinson's tremor. "In my grief, it did not even occur to me to ask her when or how she was coming", my mother explained herself to someone. My dad asks me how I was doing, if I was healthy and how my work was going. He tells no one in particular how shocked he was at how fast my aunt's health deteriorated. He asks me how I spend my free time and about the house I promised to buy him.

*

"My in-laws used to stand next to me by the phone to listen in on what I told my parents", Chitthi tells me as the clock chimes quietly at 3am. "It was a very strict household. It is still a bit like that but I also sometimes just go and do what I want" she giggles.

We talk until light begins to seep through the windows by our feet. At 5:30 am, she gets up to make tea for her husband.

*

Hour 5: Last rites happen. I am waiting for my aunt to show up and tell me what to do.

*

Hour 6: "He needs to eat", my mother tells me. She insists I take him, turning down J's offer to help. My dad and I left in the cab my mother had ready. "Both children take after their mother," my dad tells a relative over the meal, talking about me and my brother. "She is well educated, got a master's degree. I only completed high school." He then says, "My daughter's judgment of people is impeccable. It's in her stars."

*

Hour 8: I shower, after the plane ride, after the funeral. "She's upset", someone says in hushed tones pointing upstairs. AJ disappeared there right after the last rites.

*

"Do this one thing for me", my mother pleads. I agree to invite my brother to my wedding over the phone on top of sending a physical invitation. I hadn't heard from him in 5 years.

"Hello?"

"How are you?... Of course, I will come to the wedding... I am trying to build an actual relationship with dad's side of the family. Can you please invite them to the wedding? I can do it myself if you'd rather"

"I can do it"

*

3 months before the wedding: "She will come at least for a couple of hours. Can you accommodate her?"

2 weeks before: "Sorry Akka, I can't make it to your wedding"

10 days before: "I have to skip the Friday dinner because it's the only day we got tickets for Harry Potter. Can I also skip Sunday breakfast to go see AJ?"

10 days before: "You don't understand what I am going through!"

9 days before: "Happy birthday to the sweetest little girl!"

2 days after: "The train broke down. So we hired a cab to go see her"

*

"Your brother was in London last week as well. He was so busy he couldn't call you. He will be at the wedding though"

*

Hour 10: Part of the family stayed at a nearby hotel after the funeral. The men would wake up at 5 am to go to merge the ashes in the nearby river. The women would wake up at 7 am to go to the house to help with the meal preparation for the ritual.

*

Hour 17: "No, you come with your other cousins. They should be awake in 3-4 hours"

"But with you going to the house, I'm gonna be alone in this hotel room till then"

"That's fine. Come with your cousins"

I sit in a hotel room, spending 4 out of the 24 hours I spent in India, alone.

*

Hour 21: "I forgot you were here"

*

Hour 23: "Do you want something to take back to the UK?" my mother asks. "No" I say, sitting in the kitchen where I used to sit with my aunt.

*

Hour 24: My mother pushes my suitcase along as I get into the cab. Others accompanied me to the airport, even if it meant arriving way too early for their flights.

I land in London, with my body intact.
