Symrun

June 2026

I imagined having this conversation with you by the footbridge near the Crossharbour station - the one that I always stop by for a few seconds before heading to you. The one that I go back to if I felt particularly at peace after the session. The one with ambition and tall towers on one side and the quieter boats and grounded buildings on the other, all connected by water that reflected who they were. The one I couldn't help but share a view from with you many weeks ago.

I imagined having this conversation where I would ask you to bring your guitar so that I could see you play, not as someone who was trying to teach a skill but as someone who made something true and moving, often as easily as breathing, and let myself witness that truth, be moved by it.

Instead, I had to choose the lesser medium, the one where I do not have to face your piercing eyes that allow no pretense to remain. I choose the medium where I can edit, shape, will into submission my words so that I can survive what I am about to do.

I moved to London about 8 years ago because it felt like a possibility - a new place where I could shed what I no longer wanted to carry, find the freedom that eluded me, indulge in things I was deprived of and things I was yet to discover; ultimately, just find out if it was me that I couldn't run away from. I approached the experience with the intention of an engineer and built a life that is admirable by any standard - career, residency, house, marriage. I accepted when sacrifices were demanded of me, it was the price to pay for stability. I found and chose someone who loves me: a kind of certain, stable love that was absent most of my life. It was an unfamiliar feeling and I carried it with me for years, letting it shape the person that I was, blind to the invisible cost. And it was so easy to pay the cost - I was in a new country, left behind anyone who even attempted to understand me, shapeshifted in the name of belonging, to work towards goals that were the higher purpose.

Late 2024, I started shaping my wedding in my image and used it to reconnect with people who left an impact in my life - particularly my longest and closest friend Sahiti with whom I had a falling out and was on tepid water since 2016. It took a hard conversation that had brewed over years, pouring out over chai and tears at Dishoom, on a disorienting evening in Canary Wharf, for us to realise we both had a gap that existed for years, which neither of us filled - gaps that were shaped like each other. We were both learning about the new person in front of us while simultaneously feeling like we never left. It reinforced in me that if it was truly meant for you, it finds its way back to you. (And it will make your wedding guests believe you have a sister)

My analytical brain and superstitions are constantly in negotiation with each other. I believe that the universe will teach you a lesson worth learning, over and over again, in different shapes and forms, until you actually learn it. Once my major milestones were behind and I had nothing else I ran towards, the invisible cost of stability that had accumulated over months and years, started becoming apparent. You know what I mean - you wrote an article about it in 2016 observing the cascading effects that started with your grandparents.

For me, it started a chain reaction, knocking the first domino off in October 2025, when my aunt died and I decided to get estranged from the rest of my family. Because, you see, London taught me that I did not have to run away from me - and that I was made to believe that I had to, all my life, when I did not know better, by people who did not know how to receive me, all of me. Not always maliciously but the impact remained regardless. I did not know that this was what had happened until months later, when I suddenly realised how free I felt from the force that was eroding parts of me, sanding the edges it found sharp, so that I could fit into the box that had my name on it. Sometimes, other people did not have to raise a finger - I sanded myself down because I could see - I could see what would be welcome and what would make them uncomfortable. This percolated through everything - in the way I felt about myself, the narrative I had for myself, the choices I made with that foundation. When you have lived with a force like that all your life, you are a frog in water where the temperature rises so imperceptibly until you die slowly. And I had just pushed myself out of the water, before it was irreversible.

When 2026 started, I knew it was going to be a year of reckoning - the way you know when you learn to trust your instincts, even with caution and cynicism. The career I built, the marriage I walked into with my eyes open just 7 months before that, my life choices - everything came under observation. Not in a cold examination kind of way but more with a curiosity - as if I was watching myself from a third person and am fascinated to see what I did and do. What felt right and what felt incomplete. And early March, I decided to let music back in my life.

I had a fair idea of what I truly wanted and was not going to make big commitments in the name of consistency. I shortlisted people who offered a free trial and decided to book first with a guy who wrote about music, not in a "I have to write a blog" kinda way but in a lighter, less performative, "couldn't help himself" kinda way.

He would make axiomatic statements about my boots and David Lynch early on, and get evasive when I ask a simple question about his weekend. I would calibrate accordingly and he wouldn't let go. With him, I never performed, never held myself back in exchange for acceptance, never tried to make myself more impressive than I was, and I never let him get away with things. I had practice. After almost a decade in the industry, I finally had a manager I actually got along with - someone who treated me as an intellectual peer, who would stress test my ideas and I his. Catalin would openly change his mind if the argument was persuasive, hold his knowledge lightly and with humour, and taught me to read rooms. People who saw us from the outside would think we were in a heated debate - for us, it was Tuesday. I had practice and surely, I can handle an opinionated guitar teacher I met 30 minutes a week.

I don't know how it happened but those 30 minutes became a place where I was my fullest self - analytical, creative, and tactical brains finding their ground together. I was wittier, lighter, myself and the room could take it. I could send a long list of questions and was met with serious answers, unthreatened by curiosity. It had the audacity to tilt its head and call me a "Daria-coded individual" to my face. It did not need anything from me but to show up and enjoy myself. Calluses formed while I excavated parts of myself that were buried under years of conditioning. I loved the person that was emerging - a person I had written off as too much for anyone. And the guitar teacher had a front row seat, delighted by what he saw. I was used to people getting uncomfortable, terrified, insecure, or simply glossing over. Not taking delight. But this was to be expected, all the reviews said so.

What was not expected was me walking by the Potomac river with Sahiti mid-April 2026 and asking her "Are you a water person?" It was a question that came out of my mouth instinctively and surprised me. It wasn't formed from my native vocabulary, but influenced by someone 4000 miles away, across an ocean, in a living room in Isle of Dogs. I knew something had changed. I told Sahiti that crushes were just a lack of data and that it would dissipate in a month.

Except, it's been twice that time and I am still waiting for it to dissipate.

I came back from the US and things started moving towards me - slowly, deniably, but with an incredible charge that I could feel in the room. Pencil proxies threatened to fail, hallways remained unlit for just a tad longer, guitars got held in lieu of everything else that wanted to be held but couldn't be. It was to be expected, it was an intimate setting with music - the ambience did the work.

What was not expected was me walking in one day saying my bank holiday was "disappointing" - that I even thought that was an appropriate thing to do. And to be met with "How are you handling the disappointment?" and have Qawwali music playing before I enter. To have someone try to appease me for twice the length they were obligated to, concede opinion on a song they sidestepped diplomatically for weeks, and when that did not work, follow me down rabbit holes till midnight. No guitar teacher needed to do that for a student.

And you knew. You knew when you made ginger tea, with a cheeky "I wouldn't dream of it" and sat me down to waffle about your life outside our 30 minutes. "Okay, I can respect boundaries", I thought to myself. But you wouldn't let me go - as if the renewal question was absurd and the right follow-up was a confession between an alpaca and a walrus.

I tried - I took a break to let things temper, to let myself calm down, to wean myself off the need to be seen fully for the first time in my life. Except, once you are seen fully and know how that feels, you cannot unknow, in a way that stands against rational arguments and the list of "shouldn't"s. That break broke me in ways you would not know and the session after the break felt like no time had passed, while you waited for guitar to get back in through osmosis.

This is not a crush, this is not infatuation, this is not ephemeral, this is not guitar teacher and student. It is a more solid feeling, that is hugely, massively insubordinate, refusing to be dictated about the pace at which it should move.

I asked you if I was misreading in a room that was fueled by subtexts and you said this was just a student experience, while looking mortified. I let you have the narrative you needed because I couldn't bear to see you like that - I left so that you could have some peace. But in doing that, for the first time, I was not myself in that room. And what a heartbreaking tragedy that was.

The guitar lessons are true, they have been good, I have been progressing. You've also inspired me in ways that you might not have realized. Seeing you hold the identities of musician, tutor, and writer, with no hierarchy or compromise, made me believe that I could be an engineer and writer myself - it made me consolidate my writings in 30 minutes, something I hadn't been able to do for years, just so you could read them. And I am trying to write first, think later - sometimes unsuccessfully so.

But since October 2025, I've sworn to not let myself accept anything less than the truth and the truth is I love you. Not in the way that is tested by broad daylight or years of the ordinary and the mundane. But in the way that for the first time in our lifetime, neither of us feel lonely for 30 minutes a week, and feel the difference while having good people around us all the rest of the week. It's why we linger in the hallway with its leaky ceiling and text past midnight in spite of our complications, shadows, and the higher ideals we so suffer to live up to.

Commute to you is not just for guitar lessons anymore and I am not running away from that fact. It does not make my life easier, it will take a lot of work to get to the other side (if we ever do) but I am not hiding how I feel. And the fact that you need me to, means this isn't in line with the reckoning that my life has built up to so far. I understand the weight of the identity rupture this is causing. I am just not willing to sacrifice myself to make it easier.

You have given me guitar and no one can take that away from me, not even you. I will eventually find a different teacher, when I am ready. And you can text me and we can talk, when you are ready, without hiding behind the professional frame. I am not expecting everything to be resolved immediately - that takes time and we will give it that time. But I cannot unknow what I know. Whether you are willing to show up - not just as my teacher but as you, all of you - is up to you.
