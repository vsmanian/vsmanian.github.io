When it inevitably ends

April 2026

I've walked this route 6 times now. I get off the train, through the station, and look up at this building. It's not too old, not too new, not ornate, not soulless. Just a few of the windows are lit with the right shade of amber and I take a picture. It looks like it could be the cover art for 'We grew up at midnight' by The Maccabees. Or Max Richter's On the Nature of Daylight. Depending on the day, the sky, and my mood. I have 6 pictures so far.

It's not his building, just something on the way. The best part about his building is not the building.

I noticed the green in his hair two days ago, when he was standing by the door like he does every week. It reminds me of peacock feathers - not the bright part that immediately catches your attention. But the part below, the brown fibres, lustrous but overshadowed, have streaks of green sometimes - in unexpected places. They give it texture, a mischievous edge, but you have to look.

He says my name casually, playfully, often - like he enjoys the way it feels in his mouth. 6 weeks and I don't say his name once. It would make it truer than I can cope with.

"Badminton and here are the only places I drink water", I say and he laughs. I don't remember when it became so important - now it is a running joke. But when I come back after 10 days and a glass of water is ready on the table, it is also something more.

I will miss these when it inevitably ends. God, I hope it ends before I lose myself.
