When the World Slept

She was my சித்தி (Chitthi) - a word that describes "mother's sister" in a way that "aunt" never quite captures. She was a second maternal figure in my life, even during the times I didn't fully understand what that meant…

When the World Slept. A tribute to Sivapriya Jayarajan. Cross-generational bond, birthday cards, kitchen conversations, Tamil and Malayalam. She was the first family member I got to know as a person, not just a relative. Her warmth, storytelling, and kindness touched everyone she knew.

Beef Season 2 Episode 1

I had to wait to come back across the Atlantic to finally catch up. And as someone who holds the proud badge of "Not binging" against my will, I watched the first episode today…

Beef Season 2 Episode 1. Oscar Isaac and Carey Mulligan reunite. It wasn't that long ago that I watched 'Inside Llewyn Davis' and they carry the same dynamics - her being holier-than-thou but not as accomplished (+ a British accent in Beef), him talented but insufferable. The fact that this season is about 4 people instead of 2 is something my jury is still out on. Beef season 1 had a lightness about it, the levity that comes from Steven Yeun feeling alive when Ali Wong chases him. The scene with Young Mazino and Ali Wong jumping on the bed after weeks of catfishing was poetry. It might be harsh to judge one episode against an entire season but so far, the lack of whimsy is a downgrade (The only exception is the scene where Oscar Isaac has his golf club raised and the naive-in-love couple stare for a second before running away) The smarts and imagery of season 1 still exists though (the almost road crash was a nice little callback without being on the nose) One of the surprises was calling Mulligan / Crane's aesthetic sense as colonial. It does a lot - South Korea's own history with colonialism from Japan, and a commentary on how "traditional" aesthetics are viewed in a modern, multi-cultural world. It could sprawl into its own theme in the season. But there's too much already at play: commentaries and consequences of late-stage capitalism, complexities of old relationship, fragility of new ones (and the painful reminder of the US healthcare system) and 4 main characters. Is the season biting more than it can chew? Just have to wait and find out. PS: Oscar Isaac is delightful as always, even when he is an a-hole.

For What Joy

February 2026

Arrival

I've come to love reading on the train. It was an acquired taste for a woman, the indulgence in one's imagination, the temerity to get lost in another world in a public place…

Arrival. I believe that everything has its time - even books. The fact that I am reading Arundhati Roy's memoir at 32, the exact age when she wrote her first public piece, is no coincidence. Rebellious daughters of towering mothers find their way into the secret sisterhood. Reading on train makes me believe I could do anything I wanted to and that I would reach my destination, both in matter and mind. I have different books for different routes, to reflect their varied objectives. I put my head down until I reach where I am heading, not unlike life. But when the January sun seeps through the skyscrapers, though the large window that I love on trains, I can't help myself but close my eyes and let it take me.
