Was watching Joan Didion documentary when her husband has just died and she and her friend stand in front of his closet. The friend is thinking, "we need to get rid of these clothes sometime" and she asks "But what if he comes back?"

Even if the mind knows and has accepted over countless conversations with Claude, analysing it from every angle, looked for every subtext, every alternative, every explanation, if your body decides to step outside the house as if nothing can stop the routine, the momentum, you just go. You go and the train arrives at the exact moment and you keep going. I have been recently accused of meaningmaking, looking for signs from the universe rather than taking it for what it is - a coincidence, a non reproducible outcome, a luck factor, a probability. But so what? So what it was a coincidence, a convenience? Does that mean I don't get to let it play out, take advantage of it, maybe even revel in it?

I missed the stop because I was busy writing. I end up at South Quay and I am reminded of the penultimate session when it rained and I stood motionless for 10 minutes here because all that preparation, the drafts, the numbered list asking him to stay still, not move, had gone out the window, swallowed down because I saw sandals in the hallway - my hallway, sandals that did not belong to either of us.

Joan Didion talks about how finishing "The Year of Magical Thinking" was the hardest thing because as long as she wrote, she was still talking to him, savoring him, remembering him. Maybe that is what this is. Not being able to let go when that's all you can do, should do - let go because it is good for you but can't because a part of you will die. And who in their right mind agrees to an amputation peacefully?

That's the problem with reveling too much. Your body is not convinced of what your mind believes and your mind cannot let go enough to submit to the body's intuition. Maybe today was a South Quay day of what-ifs, not Cross Harbour day of wishful thinking and phantom loves.

There's also something about the journey to here being more pleasant, more indulgent, more subsuming than the destination which was a bit dry covered in a cloud of some kind of water insect. I do enjoy seeing the lights come on from the houses. The fence like reflection they create on the water.

The bridge is busier today. Mothers with prams, lovers on the bridge, women looking at me trying to figure out what my deal was, people lost in thoughts and views occupying the benches.

My Monday 8:45 really is gone in a way that I am starting to see, replaced by swans swimming straight ahead, leaving ripples that they might not be aware of. But the water knows.
